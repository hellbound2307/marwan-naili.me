A capability claim with no measurement attached is marketing. This page exists because the difference between "it works" and "it works 48 times out of 48, on this device, measured this way" is the entire difference between a demo and a result.

Every row states what was measured, how many times, with what instrument, and what the number does not prove. Where a result cannot be published — because publishing it would mean releasing live offensive tooling — the method and the limits are published instead, and the omission is stated explicitly in Limits.

D — LIMITS

Published next to the results, not buried. A number without its boundary is a claim.

One device class
The runtime figures come from a single handset model on ColorOS. Reap timing is vendor behaviour and will differ on other OEM builds. These are design inputs for this class of device, not universal constants.
Shared hardware
Cloud timings are from a free-tier shared sandbox, not dedicated capacity. They establish order of magnitude and failure mode; they are not benchmarks.
Emulated userspace
The Tor figures were measured under an aarch64 userspace emulation layer, which adds overhead not present on a native build. Treat them as an upper bound.
Not published
Payload internals, exfiltration channels and working escape primitives are not released. What is published is the measurement, the method, and the failure classes — which is the part other people can act on.
No third party
Nothing here was measured against infrastructure I do not own or have written scope for. Where a result would require touching someone else's system, it does not exist on this page.
Single-researcher bias
One person measuring their own system is not independent verification. Every figure above is reproducible from the stated method, and none of it should be taken as peer review.
